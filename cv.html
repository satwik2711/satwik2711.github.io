Proper Scoring Rules for Agentic Uncertainty Quantification

Suresh Raghu*, Satwik Pandey*, Shashwat Pandey

arXiv:2605.24756 | NeurIPS 2026 | ICML 2026 Workshops (FAGEN, CTB)
  • Reliable agent oversight requires accurate stepwise success probabilities, yet existing trajectory-level metrics do not strictly elicit them. Introduced the Trajectory Proper Score (TPS), a family of strictly proper scoring rules with extensions to censored trajectories.
  • Established theoretical limitations of AUROC, Trajectory ECE, and scalarized Brier scores. On Tau2-Bench, recalibration barely changed AUROC (0.3 SE) but improved TPS by 43 SE, exposing substantial miscalibration.
(Conference)
2026

Confidently Wrong, Silently So: Auditing Undetectable Failures of a Deployed On-Device Language Model

Shashwat Pandey*, Satwik Pandey*, Suresh Raghu

arXiv:2608.23663 | NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on On-Device Intelligence (ODI)
  • Developed a model-agnostic reliability audit of Apple’s deployed on-device language model, investigating whether failures in calibration, confabulation, and refusal can be detected without access to model internals.
  • Found that verbalized confidence was non-discriminative (AUROC 0.47) and confident errors were indistinguishable from correct predictions using surface-level features (AUROC 0.55). A black-box consistency wrapper reduced confident confabulation from 75% to 3%.
(Workshop)
2026

SELFDOUBT: Uncertainty Quantification for Reasoning LLMs via the Hedge-to-Verify Ratio

Satwik Pandey*, Suresh Raghu*, Shashwat Pandey

arXiv:2604.06389 | ICML 2026 Workshop on Failure Modes of Agentic AI (FAGEN) | Code
  • Existing uncertainty estimation methods are impractical for proprietary reasoning models due to inaccessible logits and expensive repeated sampling. Developed a single-pass uncertainty estimator based on the balance between hedging and verification in reasoning traces.
  • Across 7 models and 3 benchmarks, outperformed Semantic Entropy in AUROC (0.79 vs. 0.74, p = 0.001) at 10× lower inference cost; hedge-free traces (25% of queries) achieved 96.1% precision.
(Workshop)

Don't Blink: Evidence Collapse during Multimodal Reasoning

Suresh Raghu*, Satwik Pandey*

arXiv:2604.04207 | COLM 2026 Workshops (Actionable Interpretability, Sci-FM) | Code
  • Investigated whether reasoning VLMs remain grounded in visual evidence throughout generation, tracking attention to annotated evidence regions to identify failures invisible to text-only uncertainty monitors.
  • Discovered 53–91% decay in visual evidence attention across nine model–dataset settings. Identified a task-dependent failure regime where confident visual disengagement is hazardous on visual-reference tasks but benign on symbolic tasks.
(Workshop)
2026

Can We Trust a Model’s Forgetting? A Causal Audit of Unlearning

Satwik Pandey

Research blog, 2026 | Post
  • Investigated whether machine unlearning removes learned information or suppresses its expression, using controlled refusal adapters and activation steering on Llama-3.2-1B to assess causal recoverability across suppression mechanisms.
  • On held-out authors, the learned steering direction recovered 61% of the target-answer likelihood gap in the refusal model but widened it by 69% in the gradient-difference unlearned model, suggesting that similar behavioral signatures of forgetting can conceal distinct underlying mechanisms.
(Technical Blog)

Repair of Thought: Advancing Automated Program Repair through Dual-Model Reasoning

Satwik Pandey, et al.

Manuscript | Code
  • Developed an open-source, dual-model framework for function-level automated program repair, decoupling bug diagnosis from patch generation to improve transparency and reduce reliance on proprietary models.
  • Achieved an 83.1% plausible repair rate and repaired 215 of 522 Defects4J bugs correctly (41.2%). An automated structural, symbolic, and semantic verification pipeline achieved 95.7% agreement with human annotations.
(Manuscript)
