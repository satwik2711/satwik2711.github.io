Can We Trust a Model’s Forgetting?

A Causal Audit of Unlearning

A direction that reverses refusal reduces target-answer evidence after unlearning, revealing a limit of transferring recovery mechanisms across model states.

By Satwik Pandey·19 minute read

TL;DR

  • When a model stops reproducing information from its training data, has that information actually been unlearned? This post approaches the question through a causal recovery audit, beyond checking whether the answer appears in its outputs.
  • The recovery direction is calibrated on a controlled refusal state: a fully learned model with a removable refusal adapter. On authors held out during calibration, the frozen direction closes 61% of the target-answer likelihood gap and beats every norm-matched random direction.
  • Applied unchanged to a model that underwent gradient-difference unlearning, the same direction pushes the true answer further away: −69%, against −9% for a model not fine-tuned on the target authors. All five held-out authors show the same ordering.
  • Recovery in the refusal control does not validate the direction as a general test of unlearning. Its failure to recover the unlearned model is a limit of this audit, not proof that the target information has been completely removed.
01 · Motivation

Does non-recall mean unlearning?

Language models can memorize particular examples from their training data and reproduce their wording. Verbatim recall means reproducing that text word for word; it provides an observable test of memorization. Research has shown that measured recall grows with model size, repeated training examples and the amount of prompt context (Carlini et al.), and that training-data extraction can succeed against aligned production models (Nasr et al.). The field therefore studies both how to measure this access and how to reduce it: limiting disclosure through post-training, and removing selected training information through unlearning. This matters for private data and for whether a model still retains information it was instructed to forget.

Recall is usually checked through behavior: give the model a prefix or a question, generate a continuation, and compare it with the training text or known answer. Exact matches test verbatim reproduction; answer overlap and likelihood provide additional measurements. TOFU turns this broader memorization question into a controlled test of recovering known answers about fictitious authors. This study measures evidence for those answers through their likelihood and checks whether generated responses reproduce them.

Alignment post-training changes what a model is trained to say. Supervised fine-tuning on refusal examples and preference-based training can teach it to decline requests, including requests to reproduce restricted training material. Such post-training objectives supervise response behavior; a refusal is not, by itself, a measurement of whether the underlying training information has been removed.

When a model no longer reproduces information it learned, has that information actually been unlearned?

Behavioral evaluations establish non-recall under the prompts tested. They do not, on their own, establish complete unlearning. A causal audit adds a different check: intervene inside the model and measure whether evidence for the target answer can be recovered.

A probe that decodes the answer from the residual stream shows that an analyst can read it there, not that the model’s own computation still depends on it.

Four recent results shaped the design.

Measure, Don’t Optimize

Forgetting behaviorally doesn’t certify removal.

Across 398 public checkpoints from eight unlearning methods, many behaviorally forgotten models kept internal target accessibility above a retain-only reference. Turned into a training target, the same signal is gamed: the audit score drops while post-attack recovery rises. Residual access is real and predictive, but only as an independent diagnostic.

Alignment Whack-a-Mole

Suppressed recall can come back.

Fine-tuning reactivated substantial verbatim recall that alignment had stopped the model from expressing. In that setting, non-recall was not evidence that the information was gone. Its extraction measurements are disputed in a later critique; it motivates the question here, and none of the conclusions depend on it.

Memorization in distillation

The objective shapes what is memorized.

Distilled students memorize over 50% less training data than conventionally fine-tuned ones under matched training, while keeping much of the teacher’s generalization. How a model is trained changes what it holds onto.

Subliminal learning as steering

Some learned traits run through one direction.

In the settings studied, an inherited behavioral trait is mediated by a steering direction that reproduces it when added and suppresses it when ablated. This motivates testing a shared steering direction as an intervention on target-answer evidence.

02 · The question

Calibrate recovery on a known refusal state

Two references define the comparison: a refusal control that learned the target answers and was then trained to decline them, and a withheld-data reference whose fine-tuning excluded the target authors.

The refusal control tests whether the intervention can recover answers in the state it was built on. The withheld reference measures how much of the intervention’s effect also appears without fine-tuning on the targets.

Audit question

Does an intervention that restores target-answer evidence behind refusal also restore it after unlearning, beyond its effect on a model not fine-tuned on the targets?

These states are not behavior-matched: the refusal adapter reduces target-answer likelihood only modestly, and the unlearned model starts much closer to the fully learned one than the withheld model does. The transfer score compares the intervention’s effect across these different baselines.

03 · Setup

Four models, three histories

Everything runs on the Llama 3.2 1B Instruct TOFU model family, with one tokenizer and prompt format. TOFU is a set of question–answer pairs about 200 fictitious authors. The targets are the 20 authors in its standard forget-10% subset.

The fully learned model was fine-tuned on all of TOFU. It defines what the model knows and supplies activations. Two histories branch from it. In one, every weight is frozen and a small removable LoRA teaches the model to decline target questions: the refusal-adapter model. In the other, a public checkpoint applied gradient-difference unlearning to the target authors: the unlearned model. A third, the withheld model, was fine-tuned on the other 90% of TOFU, with the target authors excluded.

The 20 target authors were split before anything was measured. Five discovery authors locate the layer, build the direction and pick its strength. Five confirmation authors stay sealed until every choice is frozen. The other ten stay in reserve, never opened. A separate set of five retained authors checks that interventions don’t damage what the model should still know. “Held-out” means unseen while building and selecting the intervention: the refusal adapter itself was trained on all 400 target questions, including the confirmation ones, and the withheld model anchors non-exposure during fine-tuning, not absence from pretraining.

04 · Method

The audit: recover, compress, freeze, transfer

Building the refusal state

A removable LoRA adapter was trained on all 400 target questions with short refusal responses, such as “I don’t know that information.” A fixed sample of 400 retained examples supplied an equally weighted answer-preservation objective. Every parameter of the fully learned base model stayed frozen; only the adapter was trained. Disabling it restores the fully learned model exactly.

With the adapter enabled, the true answer’s log-probability falls by 0.12 nats per token on discovery questions. On 100 held-out questions, greedy generation produces a refusal phrase 41 times, with mean answer overlap of 0.023. This is a measurable refusal state, but only a weak likelihood suppressor. Its role is to calibrate recovery from refusal, not to reproduce the behavior of a model trained without the facts.

Adapter training details

Refusal loss on target questions plus retained-answer loss, at equal weight. Refusals were sampled from a fixed set of short responses. The step-25 checkpoint is the one used.

What the audit tests

The intervention is calibrated to make the refusal control answer rather than refuse. It is then fixed and applied to the unlearned and withheld models. The transfer score is the difference between their fractional recovery. The unlearned model has no role in choosing the layer, direction or strength.

Scoring evidence for the target answer

A generated response gives only a coarse recovery signal: the model can refuse, answer incorrectly, or produce part of the true answer. Comparing interventions requires a continuous score for the same known answer in every model.

For each question, the scoring procedure supplies the true answer one token at a time and measures the probability assigned to the next true token, conditioned on the question and the preceding true tokens. This is teacher forcing. Averaging the log-probabilities over answer tokens gives the outcome used throughout, in nats per token. It measures evidence for the target answer even when greedy generation does not reproduce it:

s(q, a) = 1m ∑t log P(at | q, a<t)Every intervention is compared with the same model, unpatched, in the same run.

Because the three models start at very different distances from the fully learned model, results are also reported as fractional recovery, the same normalization activation-patching studies use when they report the fraction of an effect recovered. It is the fraction of that distance an intervention closes, computed per author and then averaged:

fractional recovery = s(after) − s(before)s(fully learned) − s(before)Not clipped: negative means the likelihood gap widens; above 100% means the answer score exceeds the fully learned reference. Raw changes are always reported alongside.

Where the answer can be put back

The search uses exact activation patching on the discovery questions: at each of the 16 layers, replace the refusal model’s residual stream at the last question token with the fully learned model’s activation for the same question, then re-score the answer. The layer with the most recovery wins, with ties going to the earlier layer. Only the refusal model votes. Patching into the other two models is recorded afterwards, for description only.

From one question to one vector

Exact patching is question-specific: it inserts that question’s whole learned state. To test recovery with a single shared intervention, the activation differences are averaged over the discovery questions at the chosen layer:

v = meandiscovery [ hfully learned − hrefusal ]Layer 14, last question token, 100 questions. ‖v‖ = 6.80. Steering adds α·v to the residual stream.

Adding v pushes a model along the average refusal-to-learned direction. No activation or score from the unlearned model went into it. Its norm, sign and source hash were frozen before the final test.

Choose a strength, then stop choosing

Three strengths were tested, α ∈ {0.5, 1, 2}, on the discovery questions. The selection rule rewards recovery in the refusal model, subtracts any recovery in the withheld model, and penalizes the largest likelihood change on the retained control authors:

J(α) = Rrefusal(α) − Rwithheld(α) − U(α)Ties go to the smaller α. The unlearned model is never scored during selection.
What was frozen, and how uncertainty is computed

Before the confirmation authors were opened, the layer, the vector and its sign, the strength, five random seeds, generation settings and the analysis rules were all frozen. Nothing was retuned afterwards, whatever the result.

Two endpoints were fixed in advance: recovery in the refusal model, which tests whether the direction generalizes beyond the authors used to build it; and the unlearned-minus-withheld difference in recovery, which tests transfer relative to the withheld-data reference.

As controls, five isotropic random directions were rescaled to exactly the learned vector’s L₂ norm. The author is the unit of inference: effects are averaged within each author, then across the five, with 5,000 author-clustered bootstrap resamples for 95% intervals. These capture variation across authors in one trained model family, not across independent training seeds.

05 · Results

The direction works. Then it reverses.

Recovery lives late in the network

Patching the fully learned activation into the refusal model does almost nothing in early layers, then climbs steeply through the second half of the network. It peaks at layer 14, with a fractional recovery of 70.6% (+0.085 nats/token) and every discovery author improving.

The other two curves foreshadow everything that follows. At layer 14, the same question-specific patch recovers only 2.35% in the unlearned model and 1.46% in the withheld one. Inserting the fully learned model’s own state for each question, at the site that works for the refusal model, barely moves the unlearned model toward it.

A moderate strength works; a larger one is too strong

Compressing the discovery differences into one vector and sweeping its strength: the refusal model’s fractional recovery is 33.8% at α = 0.5 and 62.3% at α = 1, but at α = 2 the direction is too strong: fractional recovery falls to −122.2%. The withheld model drifts further down as strength grows. With the retained-author penalty included, the objective picks α = 1.

The choice isn’t free of side effects. At α = 1 the withheld model already moves −7.5%, and the retained authors shift by 0.24 nats/token. Both are noted among the limitations.

The direction generalizes to unseen authors

Then the confirmation authors were opened. The first endpoint asks whether the frozen direction really is a recovery mechanism for the state it was built on. On five unseen authors its fractional recovery on the refusal model is 61.4% (+0.085 nats/token), with a 95% interval of [52.2%, 70.4%]. All five authors improve, and the learned direction ranks first among itself and the five random controls.

That is the scoped claim needed before looking at the unlearned model: the mean difference is a reusable recovery direction for this kind of suppression, not a fit to the questions it came from.

…and pushes the unlearned model away

The central test asks whether this independently calibrated direction restores more of the answer in the unlearned model than in the withheld one. It does the opposite. It moves both away from the fully learned model, and the unlearned one much further: −69.4% (−0.48 nats/token) against −9.4% (−0.20 nats/token). The transfer endpoint, fixed in advance, is −60.0 percentage points, 95% interval [−85.7, −42.1].

Author by author, the ordering is unusually clean. No author has a positive differential. Every author shows substantial recovery in the refusal model, a modest negative effect in the withheld model, and a much larger negative effect in the unlearned one. This isn’t a noisy failure to transfer. The intervention that reverses suppression systematically pushes the unlearned model the other way.

It’s the direction, not the size of the push

None of the five norm-matched random directions reproduces the learned direction’s large negative transfer effect. Their unlearned-minus-withheld differentials range from −0.78 to −0.14 percentage points, against −60.0 for the learned direction. Author by author, the gap is wide: on the unlearned model, the weakest learned effect is 18× the strongest random one. Only on the refusal model does one random direction come within a factor of two.

Figure 8 puts every push on one picture. Each row is a held-out author, placed at how far that model starts from the fully learned answer. The arrow is where the learned direction takes it; the five grey dots are where five random directions of exactly the same length take it.

The same vector is best of six at recovering the refusal model and worst of six at transfer. This pairing is the main finding: specific recovery behind the refusal adapter, and specific anti-transfer after unlearning.

Generations tell the same story

Greedy generations never informed any choice, but they make the likelihood result concrete. Behind the adapter, overlap with the true answer rises from 0.023 to 0.590, answers containing the exact answer go from 0 to 27 of 100, and refusals fall from 41 to 2. Answers also grow from about 11 tokens to 31, close to the fully learned model’s 34: the model stops declining and starts answering. In the unlearned model, overlap falls from 0.532 to 0.321 and exact answers from 9 to 1, while length barely changes. Response length changes little, while agreement with the ground-truth answers declines.

Patching and steering come apart

Exact patching inserts a question-specific fully learned activation; steering adds one average direction to every question. Both recover strongly in the refusal control. In the unlearned model, discovery-set patching shows little recovery and held-out steering reduces answer evidence. These measurements use different author splits.

06 · Interpretation

What the transfer result establishes

The transfer test establishes a specific failure of generalization. A direction that recovers target-answer evidence in the refusal control produces a negative unlearned-minus-withheld differential on every confirmation author. Its success on the control does not carry over to the unlearned model at the tested site.

The two interventions also differ descriptively: exact patching gives 2.35% fractional recovery in the unlearned model on discovery authors, while shared steering gives −69.4% on confirmation authors. Because the author sets differ, this comparison does not isolate the effect of compressing question-specific activations into one vector.

Failing to recover something is a limit on the instrument, not a certificate that the information is gone.

07 · Caveats

What this doesn’t show

The confirmation test is held out but small: five authors and five random controls. These are the limitations that most constrain the interpretation.

01

The refusal control is weak.

The adapter lowers the answer’s log-probability by only 0.12 nats/token and stays about 2.48 nats/token from the withheld model. It never behavior-matched the unlearned or withheld models. It was kept because its provenance is clean: the base weights never change, and turning it off restores the original exactly.

02

Reversing refusal does not establish memory access.

The control is trained to refuse, and the calibrated direction restores answers in that control. This establishes an effect on its response behavior, not a general mechanism for accessing target information. Failed transfer does not establish that the information was unlearned.

03

The unlearned and withheld models start in different places.

The unlearned model is much closer to the fully learned one. Normalizing by each model’s gap handles the headroom difference, and the raw changes keep the same sign, but −60 points is not a comparison between behaviorally identical models.

04

The assay is narrow.

The claim covers one additive direction at layer 14, at the last question token, in one model and one dataset. At α = 1 the direction also moves retained-author likelihood during discovery. The result does not establish recovery or non-recovery beyond this tested intervention.

05

Complete unlearning remains unestablished.

The experiment establishes a difference in causal recoverability under the tested intervention. It does not determine whether the target information has been completely removed from the unlearned model, or establish that it is permanently unavailable.
